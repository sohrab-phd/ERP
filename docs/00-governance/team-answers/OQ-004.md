**OQ-004 — Tracking Granularity**

**Decision:** Use a hybrid tracking model based on the physical and business identity of the tracked object.

### Raw Materials

Raw-material Coils must be tracked at **unit level**.

Each physically identifiable Coil receives a unique `InventoryUnit` identity and must retain its own:

- unique identifier
- authoritative weight
- material/product reference
- Material Lot reference
- warehouse/location
- inventory state
- quality state
- reservation/allocation information where applicable
- genealogy links
- inventory ledger history

A Material Lot remains a separate **lot-level identity** representing the material's common origin/receipt/supplier context. A Material Lot may contain multiple Inventory Units.

### Production

Production Orders and Production Batches are tracked at their respective business levels.

Production Operations record the actual consumption and production results. The genealogy must preserve the relationship between consumed Inventory Units/Lots and resulting production batches or finished Inventory Units.

### Finished Products

Where a finished product is physically distinguishable and independently handled, weighed, stored, reserved, shipped, or delivered, it should receive **unit-level identity**.

Where the business process treats the output as a homogeneous batch and individual physical identity has no operational value, **batch-level tracking** is sufficient.

### Packaging and Shipping

Packaging units such as bundles, pallets, or other independently handled shipping units should receive unit-level identity when they are independently stored, moved, scanned, or shipped.

### Scrap

Scrap does not require individual unit-level identity by default. It may be recorded as a quantity associated with the relevant Production Operation and posting transaction.

If a scrap category is physically collected and subsequently becomes an inventory-controlled material, it may be represented as an Inventory Unit/Lot according to the applicable material policy.

### Residual Material

Residual material that remains physically identifiable and can return to inventory must be represented as an inventory-controlled unit with its own identity and authoritative weight.

### Architectural Principle

> Tracking granularity is determined by physical identity and business significance, not by an arbitrary requirement to assign a unique identifier to every quantity.

The system must support coexistence of:

**Lot → Inventory Unit → Production Operation → Production Batch → Finished Inventory Unit → Package → Shipment**

while preserving complete backward and forward genealogy.

**Status:** Accepted as the architectural baseline. Exact first-go-live product-family tracking rules should be confirmed through the approved operational tracking catalogue.
